# Supporting Research (external, independent of the rollout/marketing plans)

This is third-party industry research done independently of `business-rollout-plan.md` and
`marketing-content-plan.md` — included because it validates (and in a few places, complicates)
those plans, not because it replaces them. Full citations in `ASSUMPTIONS.md`.

## Benchmark: Culligan International

The strongest available benchmark for this business — 90 years of operating history, ~553 US
franchised/company-owned locations, publicly disclosed franchise economics.

- **Low royalty (1-2%), monetizes on hardware markup instead.** A structural lesson if
  ClearCrest/Fairway Filter ever licenses the model to a second territory.
- **Culligan's Platinum/Privilege annual inspection plans ($150-300/yr) are the direct precedent
  for the Clubhouse/CrestCare membership tiers** — validates that customers pay for an ongoing
  service relationship, though Culligan doesn't brand it as a distinct "certification" product.
- **Field reps are base + commission** (avg ~$116K total comp), not pure commission — the
  rollout plan's pure-commission consultant (phase 3) is a more aggressive, more
  capital-efficient structure than Culligan's own dealers use. Worth knowing if recruiting proves
  harder than expected against candidates used to a base salary.
- Franchise total investment $130K-$814K (wide range across FDD-aggregator sources, not
  authoritative) — a rough proxy for what a Kinetico-dealer route (phase 0 decision) might cost
  vs. the independent-brand route.

## Golf-course-community market validation

- **Confirmed white space**: no dedicated golf-community-focused water/air filtration company
  was found in a direct search — the strongest, most verifiable part of the "why now" thesis,
  and consistent with `target-markets-site-strategy.md`'s own read of "competitive intensity"
  across its ranked markets.
- **Real science behind the health angle, needs careful framing**: a May 2025 Mayo
  Clinic/Barrow Neurological Institute study (*JAMA Network Open*) found living within 1 mile of
  a golf course associated with elevated Parkinson's risk (adjusted OR 2.26, 95% CI 1.09-4.70),
  in a single-county (Olmsted County, MN) case-control study. This is a legitimate association,
  not proof of causation, and geographically limited — if this becomes marketing copy anywhere
  (it currently isn't, per the marketing plan's PFAS/hard-water/air-quality positioning), it
  should say "a 2025 Mayo Clinic study found an association..." and never claim golf courses
  cause Parkinson's, or cite the 2.26x figure without its confidence interval.
- **Not real, don't use it**: there is no rigorous public statistic for "% of US millionaires who
  live on a golf course." `target-markets-site-strategy.md`'s ICP framing (income/home-value
  thresholds, golf-community density as a targeting signal) is the defensible version of this
  thesis — a specific millionaire-percentage claim isn't out there to cite.
- **Florida is real, but per `target-markets-site-strategy.md`, not the launch market**: 1,250+
  golf courses, 60%+ inside HOA-governed communities, Naples highest golf holes per capita in the
  US. Independent water-treatment-industry research reached the same "Florida has the deepest
  golf-community pool" conclusion the target-markets doc did — cross-check, not new information,
  but reassuring that both efforts converged independently. Utah's case for going first anyway
  (home-field advantage, extreme water hardness, thinner specialist competition) is a real,
  defensible tradeoff, not a compromise.

## Field sales commission benchmarks (cross-check for the phase-3 hire)

- Solar/water-treatment analogs: commission typically 4-8% of contract value on company-provided
  leads, 8-12% on self-generated leads. The rollout plan's phase-3 consultant is closer to the
  self-gen end (Utah's "strong door-to-door culture," per the plan) — 10% is a reasonable
  starting point, consistent with both the researched range and `funding-model/model.py`'s
  PHASED scenario.
- Ramp curve (0/25/50/75/90/100% of full quota over 6 months) and 30-70% D2D annual attrition
  are well-sourced industry norms, not specific to this business, but worth knowing going into
  hiring: budget for real recruiting churn, not a single hire holding the seat for years.
